# Visuals and covers

## Bind each scene to what is actually being said

Build a short scene map from the approved script and current voice timestamps. For every shot, record the spoken phrase, intended visual fact or concept, source file and in-point, display crop, and source provenance. Keep this in the private project, not on the public screen.

Use event footage for that event, market footage for market activity, and actual product recordings for a demonstrated product. Attractive but unrelated office footage does not explain a specific financial mechanism. Avoid the same keyboard, skyline, or generic chart repeatedly filling gaps. If there is no verified product recording, use an explicitly conceptual visual language that cannot reasonably be mistaken for the product interface.

Aim for subject changes every 2–3 seconds, synchronized to the new spoken idea. This is an editing target rather than permission to cut words or force meaningless flashes. A longer explanatory animation should show meaningful new states; a minor zoom does not count as a fresh subject. Do not loop a short clip to fill a long sentence. Reframe or select additional source footage.

Use full-screen compositions. Keep dynamic material large and readable instead of shrinking it into half-screen windows. Mix real footage, restrained motion on real photos, and diagrams only when they clarify the current phrase. Do not use giant animated yellow words as a substitute for meaningful visuals; yellow belongs primarily to the cover and deliberate highlights.

## Cover specification

- Default video: 1080×1920 at 30 fps; cover hold about 12 frames. Use a real authorized portrait, normally the creator's approved suit portrait. Missing portrait assets do not authorize identity synthesis or borrowing another creator's face.
- Title: heavy, large, sharp yellow lettering. Preserve important English brand names such as FREYA. Secondary text: white, legible, visibly separate from the yellow title and portrait.
- Render text at native delivery resolution or higher, then downsample once. Upscaling a small screenshot is not a high-resolution title. Do not stretch the person or claim a low-resolution face has regained original detail.
- Produce 9:16 and 3:4 covers by separately inspecting crops. Protect headroom, title, secondary text, and account brand. Do not assume a center crop will work for both ratios.
- Keep text within platform-safe areas. Measure real glyph bounds; long translated strings need reflow or shorter accurate wording, not overlap.

## Persistent screen elements

Keep two understated lines at top left: “视频仅知识分享” and “不构成投资建议”, translated in localized versions. Keep the creator's account wordmark low-opacity with gentle movement if that approved template uses it. Avoid faces, main captions, platform buttons, and bottom controls.

Do not render bottom production notes such as “功能逻辑示意·非实际产品界面”, “工作场景资料画面”, or a repeating source label on every shot. Put provenance and production explanations in the delivery manifest. This preference does not remove substantive context needed to avoid a misleading claim; replace a misleading shot instead of relying on tiny print. No default progress bar or persistent chapter label.

## Asset evidence

A private manifest should retain filename, origin/link when applicable, date, usage permission or supplied-material status, transformation, and the spoken claim it supports. A stock license does not establish the truth of a depicted event. Do not export private consent records or source-account identifiers with a public skill release.
